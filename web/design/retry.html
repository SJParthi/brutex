Retry on /ingest

A failed request is named, logged and dropped. Nothing retries it. This is what exists, what changed underneath it, what can be built today without touching a crate, and the one field that stands between "buildable" and "honest".

The finding. Two ladders already retry, and neither of them can see a single instrument. A month that fails wholesale gets nine attempts; one instrument out of 773 that fails inside an otherwise-successful month gets none. What is new is that a request can now name that instrument — SpotRequest.members is honoured end to end — so the retry that was previously inexpressible is now merely unbuilt. What blocks it is one field: Failed carries its reason as prose, and classifying prose in the browser is the coupling this repository has already removed once.

2 · The gap, stated as an asymmetry

Autopilot::fail() takes an instrument, a month and a reason, puts them at the head of a list, and truncates that list at 40. It does not call classify(). It does not touch attempts. It does not schedule anything. The instrument is named, and naming is the whole of the response.

The consequence is visible on the census and reads as a contradiction. A series whose month failed wholesale can draw retrying — because /ingest/status.json reports it in flight and the page sets the retry verdict from exactly that evidence. A series that failed on its own inside a month that otherwise succeeded can never draw it, because nothing will ever put it in flight again. It settles to short or fail and stays there, and the operator's only lever is to re-run the whole window.

4 · The rule this design may not break

crates/pull/src/fetch.rs deliberately removed a blind with_retry, and records why in its own words: it "cannot tell a timeout from a decode fault by type", so it spent its whole ladder — 13.75 s of backoff — "re-asking for bytes that will be identical and will fail identically. A deterministic fault is not a blip."

So the rule is: classify, then retry only the transient class. Never loop on everything. A retry control that offers itself for a dead credential is the same defect wearing a button. 5 · What the page draws

The failure list is already on the page. What it lacks is a verdict per row and an action, and the design of both follows from section 4: the action is offered per class, never per row-because-it-is-red, and it is always a press.

The list gains a class column

Grouped by class, worst first, exactly as the census groups by verdict. A group whose class cannot be retried carries no button at all — not a disabled one. A disabled control invites the reader to find out why it is disabled; a group that states its own refusal has already told them.

One button per retryable group

"Pull these 7 again" — and it builds a member list from those seven, for the months they failed in, at the rung they failed at. Not the window. Not the target. This is the capability section 3 says is new, and it is the whole reason the design is worth having.

The halt outranks the button

The page already reads waiting_on[].halted per feed and already knows when the sweep has stopped. A halted feed draws no retry for any class: every attempt would refuse identically, and offering one would be a control that quietly does nothing.

The count is bounded and says so

MAX_FAILURES is 40 and the list is truncated. The page must print "40 shown · N more in the journal" rather than letting 40 read as a total — the same rule the census pager follows, and the reason the truncation is not a hidden one.

6 · Buildable now · needs one field · never

Buildable now — zero crate change

  • Group the failure list by month and by verbatim reason. Exact-match grouping is not classification and cannot be wrong about a class it never claims.
  • Offer a member-narrowed re-pull of a chosen group. wireBodyFor already knows how to send member=; nothing new is needed on the wire.
  • Suppress every offer while the feed is halted, and name the halt instead.
  • Print the truncation beside the count.
  • Show the reason verbatim next to each group, so the operator classifies. A human reading "PermissionException" will not press retry; a substring matcher might.

Needs one field before it is honest

  • Failed should carry its Disposition (or its Trouble) as a field, not leave it in why as prose.
  • Then, and only then, the page can label a group "will not help" versus "worth another attempt" and refuse the first by name.
  • Why it matters: the classifier already exists and runs where the whole body is in hand. Re-deriving it in the browser from a 500-character trimmed string is the exact coupling refusal_words was written to avoid — and a long vendor message pushes the name past the cut, so the browser would be classifying text the name is no longer in.

Never

  • An automatic retry the operator did not press. The sweep has ladders; the page has buttons. Two schedulers on one store is the race, not the feature.
  • A blanket "retry all failures". That is with_retry again, at a larger radius.
  • Classifying why by substring match in JavaScript.
  • A disabled retry button. State what the control cannot do; never draw a dead one.
  • Any retry count the page maintains itself. attempts and attempts_max are on the status route; a second counter in the browser is the two-clocks defect the census already removed once.
